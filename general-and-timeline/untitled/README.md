### Progress Report 1 and Entry Fee

First Progress report is due by December, year before IREC. This reports the current overall mission for the rocket such as competition category, planned motor, payload mission, avionics used etc.

Has recently been performed as a Google / Microsoft form available on ESRA's website. Templates tend to exist now for this form, but tends to be released a month to a couple weeks prior to due date.

Asks for very similar information as progress report 1, but expects more to have been done by now, due to development. Will begin to ask about planning for the competition.

### Shipping Initial Contact

Contact Must be made to the shipping coordinator atleast 1 month prior to shipping.&#x20;

This will include contact information, when the package is expected to be ready to ship (which must be atleast 1 month prior to event pickup date), dimensions of the package and weight, whether it contains dangerous goods, value and if you will be creating a carnet (YOU WILL).

Also expected is a full list of items you expect to ship, with valuation and weights. This can be somewhat rough since you wont know exact weights but aim to be within 10kg for the initial quote.

### Flight Test

Plan to flight test at the earliest opportunity available with NSWRA in case of issues later. This is typically hosted in either March or April.

This should be ran as similarly to how it will be ran at competition, with checksheets, inspections and only competition attendees assembling the rocket to find issues in the current plan and to gain more experience for the competition launch.

### Flight Readiness Review

The window for flight readiness review should open 3 months prior to launch. This will be done over zoom with your marker for your progress reports, so reach out as soon as the window opens.

This will require the presence of Dylan and Malcolm as well as all leadership for the Rocketry team working on the rocket. The flier of record should be present along with the mentor, so make sure they are also reached out to.

You should have launched prior to this meeting, as it will help your review immensely. Questions tend to be based off information you have given in your progress reports or any general concerns they may have from previous years. If you have done well in your progress reports this should be a smooth experience if prepared well for.

### Technical Report, Poster, Podium Materials, School Participation Letter

A couple months prior to launch, the final tech report, poster materials, podium session materials and the school participation letter will all be due.

This is the final and largest deadline the team must meet prior to launch, where failure to meet this in team means the team will be disqualified from the competition, and a poor submission of the Tech Report will mean the loss of many easier points. This means the Tech report should be started as soon as possible, and much effort put into its writing, editing and revision. This should be completed atleast a week prior to submission to not only allow some stress alleviation but also allow for technical difficulties either from the team or the organizers to not cause missing the competition.

### Fly to US

Prior to Competing, the away team needs to leave for the US. The time in which this is done is critical, as it allows for the team to get over jet lag, collect the rocket and account for any issues that arise if planned correctly. However, each day spend away costs the team in accomodation and car hire, so optimisation of time is important.

in 2024, the team had arrived approximatly 3 days prior to the start of the compeition, and it was agreed to be far too little time to prepare or settle in for the competition.

In 2026 the team arrived a week prior to the competition, which also allowed 2 days to settle in before the rocket had to be picked up. This also let the team run a dry run and test some things prior to competition and prepare further for its conference days, while also allowing time to settle in and relax prior to launching.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Pick Up rocket

Prior to competition you must pickup the rocket, whether it is with the sponsored shipper the competition endorses or with another 3rd party shipper.

If it is with the sponsored shipping coordinator, you will have a window of a few days to pickup the rocket from the warehouse. It is highly recommended that the team picks it up ASAP, ideally early on the first day it can be picked up so that the team can begin preparing, checking for damage and cross off needing to collect the rocket from its list.

NOTE: ESRA does not officially endorse shipping back home afterwards. However, we were able to talk to the company and have the warehouse hold the rocket for a day while the company came to ESRA's warehouse to pick up the shipment to be shipped back home. Begin talking to the company about this at this point if you haven't already!
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Conference Days

Starting on the Monday of the competition week, a couple of conference days will be held. Historically these have held mandatory meetings, judging sessions, Flight Safety Reviews, podium sessions and photos starting early in the day.

This means many people will be spread out all over the conference building at once, doing many different things at once, while always needing atleast one person at the teams stall at all times. This means some thought and planning must be put towards how the day will be ran and what everyone will be doing and where they will be at all times during the day, as some roles will mean members will be tied up in meetings, judging sessions or reviews throughout the day.

These are long days which will drain the team. Attendance is mandatory for the entire first day, but after all necessary competition items are completed on the second day, you can leave the conference. It is highly recommended to do so, since the following day will hold the competition launches, so that the team can rest and prepare prior.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Launch

Starting Early the day after the final conference day is the most important day of the compeition itself, the launch days. The team should be planning to launch on the first day, at the earliest opportunity.

This should have already been planned out months in advance through constant dryruns, writing and revising over checksheets and planning meetings in the lead up to the big day.

An early launch will alleviate ALOT of stress from the team and gives you the most control over the rocket and minimises the chance in a non-performing entry due to external factors such as weather, compeition hiccups and other things that can cancel launch days or postphone launches.

After launching, the team should see if they can recover the rocket on the same day as well.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Collect Rocket and Submit Data

The final compeition submission item is to submit any data, collect the rocket and check it into post flight review.

Once this is done the team can rest easy, as they may now do as they wish for the remainder of their stay, knowing they have no more competition requirments to fufill.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Ship Rocket Home

Prior to flying home, the rocket needs to be sent for shipping in which ever way has been organized with the shipping company, whether it be at home pickup or to meet at a halfway location or to be left within an agreed warehouse for collection.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
### Fly Home

After all this the team needs to come home.

When this happens should also be planned well, as some time should be allocated for when to ship the rocket after competition, assuming the team could not launch until the final launch day for contingency.

After arriving home the team should celebrate its performance after the closing ceremony.

A mandated time off period for the whole team should also be set so the team can relax prior to coming back in the following semester.
{% endstep %}
