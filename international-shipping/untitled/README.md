# International Shipping

International shipping can be a daunting task to work towards, as it involves working with many moving parts and meeting deadlines along the way which may not have been obvious or known at the outset. This page aims to alleviate the stress and guess work of organizing International travel as well as some of the rules and guidelines to keep in mind to make this process as smooth as possible.

### Initial Setup

One of the first things that needs to be done is to determine who you will be shipping your rocket with. As of 2026 IREC has had a recommended shipping coordinator you can work with, who you can work with to reduce the most guesswork in shipping, as they have a lot of experience in shipping rockets to IREC.

However some cases may mean the team needs to ship elsewhere or otherwise cannot use the recommended coordinators services, such as working towards other competitions or somehow being below the minimum shipping weight they require. In this case you must work out further details with your chosen coordinator to figure out insurance, services and paper work that otherwise is already known such as shipping codes and safety papers. In this case it is imperative you work with these coordinators IMMEDIATELY upon knowing you require their services and at least provide a rough list of items to be shipping along with total weight of the shipment.

In either case, you need to know what you'll be shipping and roughly how much the total freight will weigh for an initial quote and service request. The simplest way to find the weight is to create an excel spreadsheet and add each item to it to find it out if you don't have a set of scales able to weigh the entire contents of a shipping box. Just remember it just needs to be a roughly correct weight to begin the process, this is expected to change by some amount.

### Security

Unfortunately by the nature of being a rocketry team, our work can raise eyebrows and potentially require permission from Australian Defense to ship, depending on what the team decides to ship. These restrictions are difficult to find out on their own as it means trawling through paperwork made by government bodies that is by nature difficult to read and interpret. However from the teams reading of the documents as of April 2026, The team only needs to seek permission from the department of defense if its flight computers are completely SRAD and no resources exist to replicate them.

Keep in mind, that if the team DOES have SRAD flight computers and are based off a commercially available unit or there exists public resources on constructing such a flight computer such as those produced by BPS Space, these restrictions don't apply.

Do not take this as gospel as legislation can change over time, but know that wording can be tricky and be sure to check this is still correct as of reading.

### Carnet

A Carnet is the most daunting task of shipping, as it requires alot of time, money, research and coordination to get correct. The Carnet is an essential step to ship as an entity associated with the University of Newcastle, as well as providing protection of our shipping items during transport. Because of this, this item is set to alot of scrutiny and must be done correctly. With this we will step through what matter most in making a good Carnet and what exactly is needed for shipping. A copy of 2024, 2025 and 2026's Carnets will be provided for reference.

A Carnet must be requested to the NSW Office of Commerce. This office is rather old and as such has issues with their information on their website. The team has faced issues contacting them previously, as their email provided on their website is incorrect and outdated, so it is best to begin contact with a phone call to get the paperwork and instructions on how to file a Carnet.

This is typically in the form of an excel spread sheet for the shipped items, as well as a printed sheet for both the team leader and Malcolm to fill out together. The spreadsheet MUST be as accurate as possible, as this is what will be checked throughout transport. If an item is missing when it is initially shipped, the Carnet is void and if an item goes missing due to shipping, reimbursement is given only for whatever amount as provided for the lost item. This means your quantity and item value must be correct, so make sure your information is as accurate as possible.

Since this is the case, not everything is logged on the Carnet. "Consumable items" are never written on the Carnet, which may be a broader term than most expect. This covers anything that you are likely to lose or not bring back, because remember if it s not in the box but on the Carnet, your Carnet is void, and you don't want a lost spanner being the reason a $14,000 refund is not given. For this Only the most important items that are practically certain to be shipped back, in one form or another, are included such as the rocket body tubes, motor casing, Explosive Box, rigging equipment, BP equipment (case, covers, scales etc.). These items should also be carefully packaged and labeled to make sure there are no issues in identifying each item or raising unnecessary red-flags. For example Rocket body tubes and couplers become "Fiberglass tubes", Motor casings become "Aluminium Motor Hardware", Explosive box becomes "Red Tool Box" and looser items such as rigging equipment is kept in appropriately labeled storage boxes, so that the box itself is the item being logged.

{% hint style="info" %}
Remember, this is being checked by people who don't have any experience in rocketry as well as having to process many items a day. Try and make your items as easy as possible to find and identify. So label equipment clearly and try to have Carnet items as accessible as possible within each shipping box.
{% endhint %}

The price of your Carnet entirely depends on the value of what you are shipping, similar to insurance. The more valuable your cargo the higher the bond price

### Check sheets and Final Processes

After all the paperwork is cleared, you need to finalize packing your cargo into your chosen shipping vessels. Care should be take to ensure the items wont break or be damaged in transport by how they are packed and organized within the box. Packing foams, bubble wrap, cloth or smaller storage containers should be utilized where best applicable to make sure nothing is damaged.

As items are packed, you should be using a checklist of items to be packed, and ticking items off as they are placed within their box. This ensures that both you and the shipper know exactly what has been packed within the box, even if it is not on the Carnet. A blank document exists for use and is attached.

This document should be completed in full prior to shipping. The items on the list should be in order of Carnet items first, then other consumables after. The order of the Carnet items should be the order they appear on the Carnet item number list in ascending order.&#x20;

This sheet should also be signed off and completed by two different people to reduce the likelihood of errors in packing. Make sure as well that if something is packed but is not present on the sheet, the sheet should be updated and reprinted. Nothing except signatures and markings should be written by hand on the document.

Keep a copy of the filled out document and leave one within the box, in a place where it will not be damaged. Traditionally this is taped to the underside of the lid to avoid damage. The kept copy is for peace of mind and to know exactly what you shipped later, since due to the stress and density of tasks that you will be doing within this time leading up to the competition it is very likely you will forget what you packed. It also helps the team plan out what they need to buy when they get to the USA and plan out when they can do this well before picking up the cargo.

Repeat until all boxes are filled and filed. Double check ALL Carnet items are packed and signed off, a good method of doing this is by going in ascending order of Carnet items since you should have filled out the checklists similarly.

### Pickup

Once your cargo is ready, you can either arrange for it to be picked up from the university or to drop it off at their warehouse. Both are doable and have their merits but it is the least stressful for it to be picked up from the university.&#x20;

If it is being picked up from the university, let the shipper know they need a truck with a tail gate, since we do not have a truck loading zone so they need a truck with a lower-able tail gate. However EC does have a fork lift and the team has a pallet jack, so these can be used to load cargo onto the tail gate.

Depending on the shipping company they will require for either the delivery driver to pickup the Carnet or for you to deliver it to their office. Plan accordingly and hand off the Carnet.&#x20;

### Return

After picking up your cargo within the USA, you should begin working on the return process, as IREC doesn't really allow for shipping back items with their specified warehouse. Discussion should be pretty fast now that both you and your shipping company are now within the same timezone, so a quick phone call can sort things out fast.

In 2026, the team was able to get the warehouse IREC was using to be the pickup point for us, since it wouldn't be held for long. However make sure if you do plan on trying similar, you discuss this with the warehouse prior as they have no obligation to allow this. Otherwise the preferred point of pickup that year was in Dallas, which was about 12 hours away. So make sure you work on this before it is too late!

### Done

After this, you are done with shipping for the most part. All that remains is to return your cargo after the competition at a specified place and time and pay for shipping once you return to Australia. Good job!
