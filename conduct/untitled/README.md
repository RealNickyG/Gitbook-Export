# Conduct

NU Rocketry will attend and host many public events where the team will be representing both NU Teams,The University of Newcastle or even Australia as a whole. With this, it is paramount that the team knows how to conduct itself and behave as a professional engineering team and abide by both the NU Teams and University standards.

This guide aims to show both what these standards and expectations are, as well as how to make sure they are maintained and upheld by the team at large during public events.

### Standards and Expectations

Most of the standards are rather straight forward and unsurprising, however at times excitement or frustration can make members forget to hold themselves to our standards and allow them to slip. So no matter how trivial or obvious any standard is, it is essential for everyone to be told to hold themselves to it, and be enforced by the team.

Broadly the standards we expect a NU Rocketry student and NU Rocketry as a team to be:

* Helpful
* Respectful
* Professional
* Presentable
* Prepared

### Helpful

NU Rocketry is well known as a team that is happy to help others within reasonable means. This means if other teams require tools or assistance in any way that can be performed without detriment the teams performance or public image, the team is expected to help.\
This means that the team maintains its friendly and helpful image as a team, and helps build relationships that team can leverage. for example, in competition points are often granted for sportsmanship. The teams helpfulness can also build relationships with industry or other important figures as well, such as the Rocketry Association, which often helps the team due to our special relationship from aiding setting up launch days, cooking BBQ s and assisting in packing up after launches as well.

However in saying this, the team still should make sure that help provided is reasonable, that is only lend help if you are not currently essential to the team at the moment, and to not give away essential items immediately needed. For example, a Chief engineer should not help another team while they are needed to oversee flight critical assemblies and complete the necessary check sheets, but they can help after launch where they are not immediately needed. Another example is to not hand out a Allen key when its is currently or about to be needed in assembly prior to flight. Remember we are still a competition team, so the competition platform comes first, but otherwise the team should lend help wherever possible.

### Respectful

As a product of the TA environment and the stresses of both University and competition, team members can tend to develop opinions, tics or actions that can poorly effect the teams image when done in public. The team should be reminded of the expectation for them to act respectfully as expected from a professional  engineering team and they will be interacting with not only other members in the rocketry community but University high ups, students, members of the public, industry partners and other engineering companies. The conduct of the team at one event can severely impact the image of the team in anyone or all of these external parties and negatively affect the team in several ways in outlook, funding, restrictions, career opportunities and team attendance at future events, so this is extremely important to maintain.

Any team member can accidentally breach this standard, especially during competition due to the long stressful days, so it is important for all team members to understand the environment they are in and help maintain this standard by themselves and enforcing it to others regardless of seniority.

Common breaches are by jokes at either other teams, industry partners / companies or competition expense. While this can feel harmless, remember not only can other external entities make assumptions of the team based of this, but other team members can repeat these offenses and have situations spiral from a joke to a serious situation. This is especially important for senior members as this sets the expectation for junior members to follow who aren't as cautious or understanding of the broader consequences. Also be careful while in the USA, as given the current political climate and the culture differences between Australia and home, many members have jokes about the US, however forget that they now are within the US. This means everyone should ensure while in public the team remains respectful.

### Professional

NU Rocketry is a professional engineering team, meaning it has expectations to uphold to maintain our professional image. This means all the other standards should be held as well as some other rules to follow to do this.

* Team members should never post competition updates on their own social media prior to an official release
* Junior members should NEVER talk to any partners, competition officials, teams, University officials or media personnel without either a member of team leadership or a senior member, depending on who it is. When talking to other teams or people from just the rocketry community, it is fine for a senior member to be with them, who is well versed in the expectations of the team to ensure the teams image is upheld.
* Team members planning to drink heavily or go out, should NEVER do so with NU Teams / NU Rocketry / University of Newcastle apparel on such as Hoodies, Shirts, hats or Cotton drill.
* Illicit Substances are NEVER permitted at any University or NU Rocketry event, and should be reported to relevant authorities.

### Presentable

The team maintains its own respect and professionalism through the team maintaining a rigid standard for public events and competitions. These are

* ALL team members at competition or at a launch MUST wear identifiable NU Teams / NU Rocketry apparel. This makes wearing a NU Teams shirt compulsory but conditions may mean jackets or coats will cover the uniform, so steps should be taken to keep the iconography visible, such as wearing layers below the shirt or by wearing a NU Teams hoodie / Cotton drill on top.
* During conferences or expos the team should wear their NU Teams shirt at all times, along with either black dress pants or non-ripped blue/black jeans. Depending on expectations, either formal shoes or steel toed shoes should be worn. A NU Teams hoodie / quarter zip is permitted if it is cold
* All members of leadership MUST be identifiable at all times and maintain professionalism

### Prepared

The team has always prided itself on its preparedness and ability to work efficiently at competition. This means the team should be prepared for any situation that may arise, which means a lot of preparation in multiple places must be undertaken.

The team should be prepared to be able to be one of the first to launch at any competition through its use of dry runs, check sheets and testing. A prepared team is an efficient one, and will help reduce stress for the team by reducing the chance an outside influence could prevent a launch at competition or during a launch day. This also maintains the image of the team as a professional team, impressing others and allowing the team to have pride in their abilities. This point should also be reiterated, as it is a great feat to work this efficiently and the team should be proud of their work.

Planning for events should allow for almost anything, allowing the team to adjust and respond to any unexpected occurrences. For example during car trips, by having enough room for a car's passengers to fit within the other cars in the event of a breakdown, a catastrophic event has now become a smaller problem and now the safety of your members are no longer in jeopardy. This can also help in competition planning, such as planning for extra days within the US prior to competition, allowing for checks, dry runs, weather events, jet lag etc.
